## **Game Design Document: Cranial Nerve Crisis**

Project Codename: Cranial Nerve Crisis

Genre: Physics-Based Frustration Comedy / Surrealist Medical Simulator Core Inspirations: QWOP, Surgeon Simulator, Getting Over It with Bennett Foddy, Monty Python, British Public Information Films (PIFs).

## **1. High-Concept Pitch**

_Cranial Nerve Crisis_ is a physics-based comedy game where the player performs a delicate 12-part cranial nerve exam on a patient. The twist is that the controls are deliberately convoluted and the characters' bodies react with absurd, extreme physics, turning a calming ASMR-like procedure into a chaotic ballet of medical incompetence. The game's aesthetic is a nostalgic throwback to mid-2000s PC games, complemented by a unique soundscape of deadpan dialogue, cartoon sound effects, and unsettlingly calm reactions to catastrophic failures.

## **2. Core Gameplay Mechanics**

**Objective:** Successfully complete all 12 cranial nerve tests without overly stressing the patient or causing critical physical harm.

**Controls:** Each test features a unique, intentionally difficult control scheme (e.g., using Q,W,O,P for individual limb segments, mouse controls with extreme input lag, controlling patient anatomy instead of the doctor).

## **Failure States:**

- **Patient Stress:** A visible meter (e.g., heart rate monitor) increases with clumsy or painful actions. Maxing it out results in failure.

- **Catastrophic Failure:** Specific actions result in an instant, comical "Game Over" (e.g., poking an eye, inducing vomiting).

## **3. The 12 Levels: Cranial Nerve Tests**

1. **I. Olfactory (Smell):** Use individual finger keys (Q,W,E,R) to grasp a vial and arm keys (A,D) to bring it to the patient's nose.

2. **II. Optic (Vision):** Control the patient's eyeballs independently with dual analog sticks to focus on a shaky eye chart.

3. **III, IV, VI. Eye Movement:** Use a classic QWOP/OP setup to control the doctor's arm and trace a smooth 'H' pattern in the air without poking the patient's eye.

4. **V. Trigeminal (Sensation):** Use a mouse with severe input lag and acceleration to touch the patient's face with soft/sharp objects mapped to left/right click.

5. **VII. Facial Nerve (Expression):** Control individual muscle groups on the patient's face using number keys (1-0) to replicate expressions like "smile" or "frown."

6. **VIII. Vestibulocochlear (Hearing):** Use a timed power meter (SPACE) to strike a tuning fork, then use floaty Arrow Keys to place it on the patient's head.

7. **IX & X. Gag Reflex:** Control the doctor's arm (W,A,S,D) and tongue depressor angle (mouse) to navigate the patient's mouth, which randomly tries to close. This is the ultimate test.

8. **XI. Accessory (Shoulder Strength):** A two-part challenge using mouse clicks to apply pressure

and arrow keys for the patient to resist appropriately.

9. **XII. Hypoglossal (Tongue):** Control the patient's slimy, physics-based tongue with the mouse to move it from side to side.

## **4. Art Direction & Visuals**

**Aesthetic:** Early-to-mid 2000s PC game (e.g., _Half-Life 2_ , _The Sims 2_ ).

- **Character Models:** Human-like but slightly blocky and stiff, avoiding the uncanny valley.

- **Environments:** Sterile, bland doctor's office with flat, slightly blurry textures. Lighting is

- functional and bright.

- **Physics:** The core of the visual comedy. Character models appear normal at rest but reveal their extreme physics properties upon interaction.

## **5. Character Design**

Two switchable character models are available for both the doctor and patient role, creating four unique gameplay dynamics.

1. **The Skinny Guy ("The Human Noodle"):** Appears lanky but has physics akin to a ragdoll made of loosely connected noodles. Prone to collapsing, buckling, and stretching in absurd ways.

2. **The Thick Girl ("The Juggernaut of Jiggle"):** Appears as a normal curvy woman but has the physics of a human-shaped water balloon. Every action creates massive, lingering oscillations and momentum, making precision nearly impossible. She can fail by losing structural integrity and slumping into a pile.

## **6. Audio Design: "Ambient Dread"**

The audio is designed to be "relaxing" for outside observers, creating a stark, comedic contrast with the on-screen chaos. There are **no jump scares or loud, aggressive noises.**

- **Primary Soundscape (ASMR):** The dominant audio is quiet and calming. Soft whispers from the doctor, gentle rustling of equipment, and ambient room tone.

- **Dialogue (The Monty Python Approach):** Characters react to horrific failures with extreme, deadpan understatement.
  - _Patient (after being poked in the eye):_ "Oh, pardon me. I seem to have something in my eye. Do carry on."

  - _Doctor (after inducing vomiting):_ "Hmm, an atypical gastric response. Very interesting."

- **Sound Effects (Absurdist Comedy):** Physical interactions are underscored with cartoonish or unexpected sounds.
  - _Poking an eye:_ A rubber duck squeak.

  - _Hitting with tuning fork:_ A whimsical _boink_.

- **Signature Musical Cues (Memes & Nostalgia):**
  - **Entering the Mouth (Nerves IX/X):** Yello's "Oh Yeah" plays softly.

  - **Vomit Failure State:** The original PlayStation 1 startup sequence sound, creating a sense of a "cosmic reboot."

  - **Other Failures:** The Windows 95 startup sound for a "biological blue screen of death."

## **7. The Window of Distraction**

A key environmental feature designed to break the player's focus.

- **Mechanic:** Outside the doctor's office window, silent, unsettling vignettes will periodically play

- out.

- **Content:** These scenes will be visual reenactments of infamous, psychologically scarring Public Information Films from the UK and Australia (e.g., _The Spirit of Dark and Lonely Water_ , _Apaches_ ).

- ● **Purpose:** To create a powerful conflict between the intense focus required for the foreground task and the morbid curiosity of watching the silent horror unfold in the background. This reinforces the game's theme of "ambient dread" without resorting to jump scares.
